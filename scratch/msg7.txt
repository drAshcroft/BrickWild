INT-001 fix: a row shares one aisle, and that aisle is its use zone

The row rule could not place SEATS, which is the first thing its own
description says it is for -- "beds in a barracks, PEWS IN A CHAPEL, stacks in
a library, trestles in a hall".

A seat carries its use zone BEHIND it: the space you push a chair back into.
`_place_row` tested each candidate with `_fits` before the row existed, and
`_fits` requires a piece's zone to be on real floor -- so a pew backed to a
wall had its zone inside the masonry and not one bench of a row would ever fit.
Traced from a chapel that came out with an altar and nothing to sit on: the
placer reported 0 of 2 fitted on every wall, at every offset, at every count.

The fix is one line and it says what a row is. A row SHARES ONE AISLE, assigned
to every member once the row is known, and that aisle is its use zone -- the
per-piece zone is not the truth about a pew and must not be tested. Pews get
into a pew from the aisle in front, which is exactly what the shared zone
already describes.

With it, the chapel comes out as a chapel: the altar on the centre line at the
far end, two pew rows either side of it, no compromises, and plan, furnishing
and walking all clean.

Also here: the nave recipe now asks for a pitch of 0 so the rule spaces the
pews by the pew. A bench in this catalogue is 2.78 m long and the 1.2 m pitch
written for it was fiction -- `use_pitch` is never less than the piece and a
hand, so the number was being ignored anyway. Saying 0 says that honestly.
