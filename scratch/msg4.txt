GEO-002: rooms a rectangle cannot say

A room may carry an `outline`, and when it does the outline is the truth:
`rect` stays as its bounding box so every rectangle-shaped rule still has
something to measure. A room WITHOUT one is the four-sided case, which is every
room in every house, and that is the point -- the change is additive, and the
house, shop and hotel families come out unmoved.

An outline is the CLEAR FLOOR, not a partition centre-line. A rectangular room
is cut out of the interior and shares half of each partition with its
neighbour; a polygonal room is not produced by cutting, so there is no shared
partition to give half of, and what you draw is what you walk on.

  HouseGeometry.room_walls     one wall per edge for a shaped room, and the
                               fixed front/back/left/right labelling for a
                               rectangle -- that order is a LABELLING and not a
                               traversal, and "the hearth is on wall 2" has to
                               keep meaning the left wall
  room_floor_poly, room_area   the outline, and its true area
  polygon_runs, shell_runs     the wall centre-lines the builder raises
                               masonry along: the outline pushed out by half a
                               wall, exactly what exterior_runs does to the
                               site rectangle
  HouseBuilder                 walls per edge and a floor slab through the
                               outline, via MeshKit.slab_poly
  HouseFurnisher               every corner of a piece inside the outline, not
                               merely inside the box round it
  HouseNavCheck                rasterises the outline, so the corners a
                               chamfer cuts off are wall and nobody stands in
                               them
  HousePlanCheck               shaped rooms compared by polygon intersection;
                               the "floor nobody owns" sum applies only to a
                               storey of rectangles, because the corners an
                               octagon cuts off are masonry and that is what
                               makes it a tower

Four rules turned out to be measuring the box rather than the room. Every one
was found by building an octagonal tower and looking at what it said -- not one
of them by reading the code:

* the window rule recognised a wall only by its position on the interior
  rectangle's edge, so every window on a diagonal was "in a partition";
* `_back_gap` measured to the bounding box, and reported every piece in the
  room as standing a metre and a quarter off a wall it was flat against;
* `_place_against_wall` took absf() of the wall normal to get its along
  vector. Right for the four walls of a rectangle, meaningless on a diagonal,
  where it walked the piece off the wall entirely;
* `_stair_spot` set the stairwell down in a corner the octagon cuts off --
  masonry -- giving a landing nobody could walk to.

The corrected arithmetic gives identical results for all four rectangle cases,
which is the claim that matters, and houseqa is how it is held: every affinity
rate is unchanged to the individual placement -- corner_clutter 636/666,
sconce_pair 290/296, chandelier_over 45/45, workbench_daylight 51/51,
bed_window 276/276, shelf_over 39/46 -- with all five styles at 0/24 defects.

`harchetype` gains the demo GEO-002 asks for: a round tower at two sizes, one
octagon to a storey over three storeys, asserting eight walls a storey, no
furniture outside the outline, and the whole of HouseQA. It lives in the suite
rather than in the archetype table because the archetypes all come out of
HouseGenerator, which partitions a rectangle -- the planner does not lay out
shaped rooms yet, and what this delivers is the representation and every rule
that reads it.
