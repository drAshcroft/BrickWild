GEO-003: buildings round a yard

`HousePlan.courts` is a list of holes -- {rect, storey, outline?}. A court is
FLOOR to the walk grid, SKY to the roof and OUTSIDE to the daylight rule: a
window onto a courtyard is a window. The rooms and the courts together tile the
footprint, because what a court takes is not floor nobody owns, it is floor
that belongs to the weather.

It is what a monastery, an inn with a yard and a caravanserai are, and none of
them can be said with rooms alone.

`qa/court_check.gd` says what makes a courtyard building, in four rules -- and
what makes one is not that it has a hole:

  SKY         nothing is built in the court, no furniture and no stair. The
              plan check already forbids a ROOM there; this catches a yard
              filled with the hall's tables, which is a hall with the roof off
  INWARD      more windows onto the court than away from it. A courtyard
              building turns its back on the street, and one that put its
              glass outside has the plan inside out
  RING        every range standing on the yard has a door onto it
  PROPORTION  the court is a room, not a light well and not a field: narrow
              side at least 3 m, at least a twenty-fifth of the footprint, and
              a warning when the ranges stand too tall for sky to reach the
              ground

Three rules already there had to learn that a court JOINS rooms rather than
separating them, and each was a real gap rather than an accommodation:

* `HousePlan.door_graph()` joins every range that opens onto the same yard --
  you walk out of one door, across the paving and in at the other. Without it
  four ranges round a hole are four buildings, and `connected` said so four
  times over.
* `privacy` reads the same graph and followed.
* `doors_in_line` skips two doors facing each other across a yard. That rule
  is about a house you can see straight through from the street; a cloister is
  meant to look like that.

The builder roofs each RANGE rather than the whole box -- one sloping plate
falling from the outer wall in to the courtyard eaves, which is the only roof
shape that leaves the yard open -- and lays the floor in four bands round the
hole, with the court paved a hand's breadth down. The nav check rasterises the
court as floor, so the walk goes out of the hall, across the yard and in at the
kitchen door.

New `court` suite: each of the four rules shown FIRING on a plan that breaks
it, because a rule nobody has seen fail is a rule nobody has tested, and then a
hundred generated courtyard houses judged by plan, furnishing, walking and the
court rules together. 105 checks, 0 failures, 0 of 100 houses with defects.

The sweep earned its keep straight away: five houses put a bed in the hall,
because a four-range house with nowhere to sleep sleeps in its hall -- and the
hall here is a narrow range with windows on BOTH long walls, street one side
and yard the other, so the bed is under one whichever wall it takes. The back
range is a bedroom now, which is what such a house actually has.
