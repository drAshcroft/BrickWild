CAS-011: the keep becomes a HousePlan

`CastleGenerator.keep_plan(spec)` returns the keep as three or four stacked
storeys of one room each, in the keep's own frame. It is the one castle
building the house harness already knew how to describe -- stacked levels with
a stair against the wall is exactly what `HouseSpec.storeys` and
`HousePlanner._add_stair` mean -- so the keep borrows the harness whole rather
than growing a second one.

  storey 0   the store: entered from the bailey, and BLIND. No windows at the
             foot of a keep; that is the point of a keep.
  storey 1   the hall, up a stair over that store
  storey 2   a chamber, on a four-storey keep
  the top    the lord's chamber, a new kind in HABITABLE and SLEEPING, with a
             bed and a fire of its own on plan.hearth.wall

What it could NOT borrow is the house's idea of what belongs on which floor,
and that is the whole reason `KeepSpec` exists. `HousePlanCheck`'s
`upstairs_programme` rule keeps a dwelling's hall and its one hearth on the
ground floor: right for a farmhouse, wrong for a keep. The rule exempts any
spec that can answer `room_program` -- the same door a shop and a hotel go
through -- and answering it is the whole of what a KeepSpec adds.

Three things in the shared harness had to give, each for a stated reason:

* `HouseGeometry.MAX_STOREYS := 4`. The checks clamped storeys at three, so a
  keep's top floor would have been reported as an invalid storey rather than
  walked. Each family still clamps itself to what it is; a house is three.
* "A house with no bedroom sleeps in its hall" was asked as
  `has_kind(&"bedroom")` in three places -- the furnisher and both checks --
  and all three were putting a bed in the keep's hall, because its lord sleeps
  in a CHAMBER. They ask `HouseGeometry.SLEEPING` now, and agree with one
  another.
* `lords_chamber` joins MIN_AREA, MIN_SIDE, HABITABLE, SLEEPING, the furnish
  check's REQUIRED and the furnisher's RECIPES.

Two things were found by measuring the sweep rather than by assuming:

* GLAZING. One cottage casement to a wall left a 400 m2 keep floor too dark to
  live in (114 daylight warnings); a ribbon of them along every wall did not
  fix that AND left nowhere to put a bed that was not under a window (122
  bed_window warnings, one per keep). Castle windows -- 1.4 m wide, tall, five
  metres apart -- do both jobs, and the lord's chamber keeps one wall BLIND,
  the one facing the fire, because that is where the bed goes. 253 warnings
  became 17.
* SIZE. No plan under 3.2 m across, and none over 36 m: the biggest keep ever
  built is the White Tower at 36 x 32 m, and a fortress here throws up a keep
  mass sixty metres across with four thousand square metres to a floor. That
  is a block the massing draws as one volume, not a tower anybody lives up. It
  keeps its mass; what it does not get is an inside.

The `hall` suite becomes `interior` (`CastleInteriorSuite`), since it now
sweeps two hundred keeps beside its two hundred halls. `CastleSuite._keep`
asserts the arrangement on all 84 canonical ranges: the blind foot, the hall
up a stair, the lord at the top with his bed and fire, one stairwell against a
wall chaining every storey, and nothing left unreachable from the keep door.

castle 260, castle massing 97, castle landmark 92 (TowerCheck included),
castle interiors 407, house 123, assets 481, multistory 210, shops 14, shop
archetypes 52, hotel 6, hotel landmark 3, church 92, normals 90, massing 90,
blueprint 90, landmark 32: all green. `harchetype` keeps its one pre-existing
failure (one_room_cottage at scale 1.00, filed as e7607125).

31 of the 84 canonical castles have a keep; 122 of 200 across the sweep, of
which 114 are four storeys.
