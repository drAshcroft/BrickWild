CAS-012: the bailey becomes a yard

A castle was a village that happened to have a wall round it. The bailey here
held the keep, the hall and the chapel and nothing else, which is a picture of
a castle nobody worked in: no stable for the horses that got you there, no
kitchen away from the hall it feeds, no smithy, no store, no well.

`CastleGenerator.bailey_buildings(spec)` fills it, and fills it with SHOPS --
the shop family already knows how to plan a stable, a cookshop, a smithy and a
store, so the bailey invents no building kinds of its own. A castle gets the
stable and the cookshop; a fortress adds the forge and the store, because a
garrison that size cannot send out for either.

They stand along the SIDE WALLS, marching back from the gate, turned a quarter
so the front looks across the yard rather than up it. The middle stays empty on
purpose: `CastleGeometry.gate_axis_strip()` is the way from the gate to the
keep -- the gate opening plus a wagon either side -- and a yard built across
the middle is a yard you cannot cross. `bailey_well()` sinks a PropKit well in
the open ground nearest the centre, six metres clear of everything built.

Two shared rules had to learn that a courtyard building is not part of the
fortification, and both learned it the way an existing rule already worked:

* `MassRules.gaps` takes a `free` list, as `grounded` already takes `carried`.
  That rule is about a mass that FLOATS -- a buttress reaching nothing, a roof
  over no wall. A building standing on its own in a yard is not floating; it is
  a separate building, and `grounded` is the rule that says it must stand on
  something.
* `CastleQA`'s `connected_mass` seeds its flood from each free-standing
  building as well as from the curtain. It cannot skip by name, because it
  works on voxels, so instead every grounded component gets a seed. Geometry
  attached to nothing at all is still unreachable from all of them, which is
  what the rule was for.

`CastleMassingCheck` gains `bailey_clear`, measured over the LOGGED masses and
not over the layout that produced them: a layout pass that agrees with itself
and disagrees with the builder is exactly what it is there to catch. It earned
that in the writing -- three real disagreements in a row:

  the layout kept 0.5 m from the curtain where the rule wants BAILEY_CLEAR
  (137 failures), and

  on a polygonal enceinte the curtain slants INSIDE the inscribed rectangle by
  its own thickness, so a building set against the box clipped the wall behind
  it (10). `bailey_rect` is where a RANGE goes -- built against the wall on
  purpose -- so a free-standing building needs the box pulled in by a wall
  first. Reaching for `ward_edge_x` instead made it worse (58), because that
  helper returns the OUTER limit a range may reach, not the inner one a yard
  building must keep off.

The castle massing suite gains `_bailey_fixture`, which asserts both
directions: silent on a properly laid out bailey, and catching both a yard
building shoved out through the curtain and one parked across the gate axis.

castle 260, castle massing 100, castle normals 84, castle landmark 92, castle
voxel QA 84, dressing 401557: all green, 0 failures.
