The October Commission

Decaying buildings, haunted mansions and the castles of dark lords — a proposal for BrickWild

The proposal, in one sentence: decay is a pass, not a style. We do not write a "ruined church" generator, a "ruined cottage" generator and a "ruined windmill" generator. We write one seeded, logged decay pass that every family can run on its own plan. Then we add the two showpieces that decay alone cannot make: the Hollow Manor (a Second Empire haunted mansion) and the Dark Lord castles.

You asked whether the mesh rules can be loosened. Yes, but carefully. A ruin may have holes in it. A ruin may not have stones floating in the air. The rule is: every defect must be deliberate and must be written down. The QA checks accept a defect that is recorded in the decay log and fail one that is not. The details are in the rules of a good ruin.

The decay dial

A toy version of the pass. Move the slider and the house goes through the six stages real buildings go through, in the order they go through them. Damage starts where water gets in, at the roof and the openings, and the walls go last. The seed fixes which slates fall. The dial only sets how many.

Three families of dread

I. The Decrepit

decay passevery kind

Any existing building, aged. The cheapest of the three, and it gives us the most buildings.

  • Abandoned cottage: boarded up, the roof caved in, the chimney still standing.
  • Ruined abbey: a roofless nave with the gable walls standing and empty tracery, like Tintern.
  • Dead windmill: the new windmill family with two sails snapped off.
  • Broken bridge: the middle span collapsed into the river.
  • Slighted castle: breached curtain walls, roofless towers, crenellations with gaps.

II. The Hollow Manor

mansardturretrich house

The Psycho house and the Addams house: a new house style built on the rich-house ornament, plus two new roof pieces.

  • A mansard roof with iron cresting and a widow's walk.
  • A corner turret with a candle-snuffer roof.
  • A wraparound porch that sags, a bay window, and tall narrow sashes.
  • Exactly one lit window, in the attic. Never more than one.
  • Inside: a portrait gallery, a library, a ballroom with a chandelier, furniture under dust sheets, cobwebs in the ceiling corners.

III. The Dark Lords

style: dark4 variants

The dark castle already exists: a ridge plan with one spire keep. It needs a palette, some lights and three companions.

  • Black basalt with red-lit arrow slits that glow against the walls.
  • A lava moat: the moat skin, coloured orange and emissive.
  • spike merlons (they already exist), braziers, a skull keystone, tattered banners.
  • A portcullis with teeth. A road lined with pikes.

IV. The Crooked

slice warpevery kindjank

Deliberately janky buildings, with walls that are angled on purpose: Burton, Seuss, Caligari, the Crooked House at Sopot. It is the second dial on the drawing above, and it does not depend on decay. A crooked house can be brand new.

  • One affine per floor line. Each storey has a 2D shear, twist and flare. It is linear between floor lines, so the walls kink at every floor. That gives the angular look rather than a melted one.
  • Floors stay level. The warp only moves things sideways at a given height. The planner, furnisher and walker all work on a level floor, so they run unmodified, and props keep standing upright.
  • It cannot tear. A continuous map keeps a closed shell closed and every part attached. That is why the mesh rules hardly need relaxing here.
  • Per-mass warps come later. Every tower in a castle bends its own way, chimneys corkscrew, and the tips of witch-hat roofs curl.
  • Presets: the Seuss stack (each storey smaller and turned), the Burton tower (thin, tall, kinked twice), and the sinking cottage (it leans one way only, as if the ground had given).
  • It works with decay. A crooked house that is falling down is the best kind.

The four dark lords

The rules of a good ruin

We are loosening the mesh requirements. We are not removing them. A defect that was chosen looks decrepit. A defect that was not chosen looks like a bug, and in October players will notice the difference. Every decay operation writes one row to a new decay_log (the cause, the part, the triangles removed or moved). Each check then reads that log in the same way the furnishing check already treats a recorded removal: as a warning, not a failure.

How it is built

Decay on the plan, then on the mesh

There are two layers, and most of the work belongs to the first:

  1. Plan-level (before the build): which roof bays exist, which floors have fallen, which windows are boarded, which merlons are gone, where breaches cut. The builders then emit coherent geometry for a ruined plan. This keeps every log true without exceptions.
  2. Mesh-level (after the build): vertex-colour grime, moss on surfaces that face up or north, soot above openings, ridge sag, ivy sheets, cobwebs, rubble cones. Each of these is logged.

The entry point is core/decay/: a DecaySpec (amount, age profile, climate) and a DecayPass with one adapter per family. The pass seeds from hash("decay|kind|seed"), so it never disturbs the parent stream.

API shape

Recommended: decay becomes a request field that every kind accepts, so it is not one more style to sweep.

BuildingRequest.decay = 0.6
BrickWild.generate(&"church", seed, {decay = 0.8})

The Hollow Manor is a house style, the same way rich is, so the planner, furnisher and walker are used unmodified. One warning, from the rich-house notes: a new entry in HouseSpec.STYLES is added to every house sweep automatically. That is free coverage, but it adds to sweep time.

The dark-lord variants are dark sub-plans chosen by seed: needle (today's), crag, sunken, floating (the existing sky plan, chained to the ground).

Decided, 3 October

  1. Decay and jank are request fields on every kind. They are not styles, so they never multiply the sweeps.
  2. PG-13. Skulls, chains and empty pikes, and nothing on the pikes.
  3. Order: the Crooked (done), then the decay pass, then the Hollow Manor, then the Dark Lords as the finale.
  4. Mansion packs are guides, not assets. We extract them outside the repo, study them and take numbers from them. We take no files.
  5. Deliberate jank is in: walls angled on purpose, kinked at the floor lines.
